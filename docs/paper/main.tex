\begin{abstract}
Machine-learning phishing detectors are trained on a snapshot of the threat
landscape and begin to decay the moment they ship: in our setting, a
leakage-clean detector scoring $98\%$ weighted $F_1$ in-distribution falls to
$\approx\!63\%$ on temporally fresh phishing. The standard remedy --- route
low-confidence samples to human analysts and retrain --- scales with the threat,
not with the budget. We present DRIFTGUARD, a closed-loop phishing detector that
repairs itself under drift with \emph{no human labels} and a small, predictable
LLM budget. DRIFTGUARD detects drift from the \emph{input} distribution rather
than from model confidence --- which a well-calibrated detector keeps high even
as it fails --- spends its budget on samples that are both uncertain and aligned
with the measured drift, and queries a large language model as an on-demand
oracle for gold labels derived from cleaned HTML, the opinions of two frozen
base detectors, and OSINT-derived domain intelligence. Retraining on the full
union of its seed corpus and every acquired label, DRIFTGUARD recovers nearly
$18$ points of weighted $F_1$ on fresh phishing ($0.63\!\to\!0.79$, five-seed
mean) using $\sim\!1{,}250$ selective queries at a cost of a few dollars, with no
measurable forgetting. Two design choices make this recovery trustworthy and
yield a counter-intuitive finding. First, a single cross-dataset statistical
gate sterilizes the feature space, rejecting both classic in-distribution
shortcuts and temporally leaky OSINT features under one rule, so every reported
gain reflects the phishing phenomenon rather than a dataset artifact. Second, the
oracle's imperfection is an asset, not a liability: a class-asymmetric verifier
that filters its label noise yields \emph{no} improvement on drift recovery and
consistently \emph{degrades} out-of-distribution robustness, because the filtered
noise was acting as an implicit regularizer --- so DRIFTGUARD keeps every paid
label. We further benchmark six LLM providers as oracles and stress-test a
label-free health monitor against live prompt injection, delimiting what
self-repair can and cannot guarantee in deployment.
\end{abstract}
